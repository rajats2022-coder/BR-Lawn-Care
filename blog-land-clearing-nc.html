What counts as "land clearing" in Eastern NC

The term covers a wide range of jobs. On the residential side, it's usually one of three things: reclaiming a lot that's grown over with brush and briars, clearing a property line that's been taken over by fast-growing trees and vines, or prepping a raw lot before building. Commercial and rural jobs are often larger — multiple acres of scrub, overgrown agricultural land, or timber parcels that need brush and stumps removed before development.

Eastern NC's warm, wet summers mean vegetation comes back fast. A lot that was cleared five years ago and left unmanaged can be dense thicket again. That's normal, and it doesn't change the approach — it just affects how much material needs to move.

What affects the price

The main cost drivers are size, density of vegetation, tree diameter and count, access to the site, and what happens with the debris. A half-acre with light brush and small saplings is a different scope from a half-acre with mature water oaks and a decade of vine growth.

  • Lot size (square footage or acreage)
  • Density — light brush vs. heavy timber
  • Stump count and diameter
  • Access (can equipment get to it?)
  • Debris haul-off vs. pile and burn (where permitted)
  • Grading required after clearing

Photos are the fastest way to scope a clearing job. A few shots showing the density, overall size, and any large trees tell us most of what we need to put a number together.

What a complete clearing job includes

  • Brush and vine removal to bare ground
  • Tree felling and limbing
  • Stump grinding (below grade)
  • All debris hauled off or consolidated to spec
  • Final raking and site cleanup
  • Optional rough grading after clearing

We don't leave piles of material on the property unless that's specifically what the client wants (for burning or chipping on-site). A cleared lot should look cleared.

Pre-build site prep

If the goal is a building pad, driveway run, or lot ready for a home or structure, clearing is just the first step. After clearing we can rough grade the area, establish drainage paths, and remove stumps deep enough that concrete or foundation work won't be affected by root decay. Let us know the end goal and we'll scope accordingly.

What to send for a clearing estimate

Property address or GPS coordinates, photos from a few angles showing the density and any significant trees, an estimate of the size (even rough — "about a third of an acre" or "the back half of a ½-acre lot"), and what the cleared site will be used for. We return estimates within 24 hours, usually same-day.
