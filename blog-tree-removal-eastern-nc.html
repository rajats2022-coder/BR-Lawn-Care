What drives tree removal cost in Eastern NC

The biggest factors are size, lean direction, proximity to structures, and how much wood needs to go. A small to medium tree in an open yard with good access is straightforward. A large oak leaning toward a fence or roofline is a different job — more time, more careful rigging, more cleanup.

Eastern NC has a lot of mature longleaf pine, water oak, and sweet gum on residential lots. These grow fast and drop heavy wood. After storms, downed trees or trees with significant root damage are common requests across Pitt, Edgecombe, and Craven counties.

What's included in a BR tree removal

  • Full tree felling or selective crown removal
  • Limbing and sectioning on site
  • Stump grinding below grade (included when requested, quoted separately otherwise)
  • Complete debris haul-off — no piles left behind
  • Raking and cleanup of the work area
  • Logs split on site or hauled off to spec

We don't leave a yard full of brush and call it done. The job isn't finished until the property looks like the tree was never there — or at minimum, cleaner than it was when we arrived.

Dead trees and storm damage

Dead trees are actually easier to quote than living trees in some cases — there's less canopy to manage. The concern with dead trees is how far the decay has progressed, which affects how safely the wood can be rigged and brought down in sections. Photos of the base and canopy help a lot here.

Storm damage calls — split trunks, uprooted trees resting on fences or rooflines — move quickly. Send photos of the situation and the address and we'll get you a number fast.

Stump grinding: when and why

Most people want the stump gone when the tree comes down. We grind stumps below grade — typically 6 to 10 inches deep — and fill the void with the grindings. The area can be seeded or sodded after it settles. If you're planning to build, pour concrete, or install a new landscape bed over the area, let us know and we'll grind deeper.

What to send for a tree removal estimate

Photos of the tree from ground level and from a distance showing the full height. Note the diameter if you can estimate it. If it's close to a structure, include a photo showing that proximity. List the approximate number of trees if it's a multi-tree job. We typically reply the same day.
